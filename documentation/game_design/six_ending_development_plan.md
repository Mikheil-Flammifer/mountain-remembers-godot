# Six-Ending Development Plan

## 1. How to use this plan

This plan develops six endings for a 2D RPG about Aluda Ketelauri, based on Vazha-Pshavela's poem. It stands on its own and replaces the older plan in the first tab wherever they differ.

**Brief for the AI receiving this plan**

- Develop plot, scenes, dialogue and quests inside this structure.
- Keep the six endings and the path rules below.
- **\[Poem\]** marks events from the poem. **\[New\]** marks game invention. **\[Changed\]** marks a deliberate change to the poem. Unmarked design details, routes and scenes are also new.
- Keep the labels. Never present new lines as Vazha-Pshavela's.

### The decisions

1. **The hand, after the duel:** spare it or cut it.
2. **The dream:** lower the dagger, or strike Mutsal. Striking means accepting the old law.
3. **The council:** if the hand was spared, speak the truth or hide it by staying silent. If it was cut, there is nothing to hide.
4. **The arm:** if spared, refuse or accept the arm Mindia brings. If cut, give it up or hang it. Accepting always means hanging it on the tower.
5. **The feast:** sacrifice the bull for Mutsal, or hold back.

The council decision supports the dream choice: it settles whether a secret exists.

**\[Changed\]** Aluda has the dream at a night camp on the way home, before facing the village. In the poem, he tells of it after the village has heard his account.

### Which ending the player gets

Check the bull first. Every path leads to exactly one ending.

**If Aluda sacrifices the bull**

- Hand spared, arm refused: **1. The Exile**
- Hand spared, arm accepted: **2. The Broken Trophy**
- Hand cut, whatever happened to the arm: **3. Late Repentance**

**If Aluda holds back**

- Spared, struck in the dream, stayed silent and accepted the arm: **4. The Silent Secret**
- Cut, struck in the dream and hung the arm: **5. The Old Law**
- Every other combination: **6. The Hand on the Tower**

The feast locks the route. Player skill after that changes who is hurt, what is saved and how people part. It does not change the route's name.

## 2. Seasons, locations and cast

### Seasons

The [poem](https://poetry.ge/poets/vazha-pshavela/poems/2606.aluda-ketelauri.htm) shows green grass and dew around the raid and duel. It has a shrine festival, for which it names no month. The exile takes place in snow, wind and avalanches, with hungry wolves. The game calendar below is **\[New\]**, apart from those conditions.

- **Late summer:** prologue, raid, duel, night camp and council. Passes are open.
- **Early autumn:** the arm and the days of preparation.
- **Late autumn:** the shrine feast.
- **Early winter:** exile as heavy snow arrives, or the first guilty or secret-filled months in Shatili.
- **Deep winter:** travel is dangerous. Dangers are cold, avalanches, hunger and wolves.
- **Late spring:** paths reopen. Raids and revenge become possible again.

For reference, snow blocks [today's road to Shatili from about November to May](https://wander-lush.org/visit-shatili-travel-guide/). This does not prove how every old footpath behaved.

### Locations from the poem

- **Shatili:** towers, flat roofs, hearths and the entry below the cliffs. The sun reaches it late.
- **Sheepfolds and high pasture:** the alarm, tracking and later raids.
- **Arkhoti heights:** where the village expects the raiders to cross.
- **Rocky crags:** the pursuit and the duel.
- **Imeda's fort crags:** on Aluda's road home. Avalanches come down here in winter.
- **Ardoti water:** recalled in the poem's lines on bloodshed. Use it as a ford.
- **Shrine ground:** the feast. Aluda serves the Gudani Cross. The exact feast ground and its layout are game decisions.
- **The gorge:** where Mindia throws the refused arm.
- **The winter ridge:** the exile road. The poem names no destination.

### Added locations \[New\]

- **Night camp** under a rock shelter: the dream.
- **Anatori crypts** and **Mutso:** real places used for new side stories.
- **Stone hut** below the ridge: shelter during exile.
- **Kist hamlet:** fictional, beyond the ridge.

### Cast

**From the poem**

- **Aluda:** respected warrior who has taken many enemy hands.
- **Mutsal** and his brother (the poem does not name the brother).
- **Mindia:** fearless friend with a red horse. He seeks proof.
- **Ushisha:** old man who asks for Aluda's account.
- **Berdia:** khevisberi, the priest-elder who guards shrine law.
- **Family:** mother, wife Lela, children, a brother, three nephews and a sister. At exile, the poem shows one walking ahead and five following. Keep that party of six, and have the writer define who they are.
- **Young Khevsurs:** they mock Aluda.

**Added \[New\]**

- **Gela:** voice of the young men.
- **Zezva:** boy who admires Aluda's trophies.
- **Sanata:** herb woman.
- **Gigia:** keeper at Anatori.
- **Aiza:** Mutsal's sister, who wants her dead brother treated with honor.
- **Elmurza:** Mutsal's kinsman, bound to revenge.
- **Mutsal's young nephew:** shows how a feud passes to children. He needs a researched name.
- **Murana:** dog. If fed early, she is loyal to the family.

## 3. Skill gameplay

The player performs the story: tracking, shooting, fencing, climbing, protecting and holding back. The same skills return later with a different meaning.

### Core skills

- **Tracking:** read hoofprints, bent grass, wet stones, birds and sheepdogs. Good reading earns a better position. Mistakes mean a longer road or an ambush.
- **Rifle fighting:** reload in ordered physical actions: powder, bullet, ramming and priming. Do it in cover while listening to the enemy reload. Powder is scarce, rock cover breaks and wind affects long shots.
- **Sword and small shield:** high and low guards, timed blocks, feints, footing and stamina. Wild attacks leave Aluda open.
- **Flat-of-blade fighting:** for village quarrels. It draws on Khevsur ritual fencing, where fighters [touched with the flat or spine of the sword](https://kogenbudo.org/guest-blog-martial-arts-of-the-kevsureti-by-mike-cherba/). The goal is control. A woman's thrown headcovering stops the fight, a custom the source describes across the region. Using it in these village scenes is our design.
- **Bow and hunting:** read distance and wind. Learn it with Mindia.
- **Climbing:** choose holds, manage grip and carry loads. A burden changes balance and speed.
- **Mountain survival:** break a trail, hear avalanche warnings, find shelter and keep the slowest person moving.
- **Night watch:** use sound and shadow to follow, hide or intercept without killing.
- **Calls to allies:** short orders such as cover, hold, move and stop. Trusted allies answer faster.
- **Restraint:** keep a guard or aim steady through insults and feints without striking.

### Decisions made through actions

Story decisions have no pop-up answer list. The world waits while the player acts.

- **Hand:** draw the knife and complete the act with a deliberate hold, or sheathe it, cover Mutsal and walk away.
- **Dream:** take the offered dagger. Keep pushing it forward, or turn it aside and set it down while the dream resists.
- **Council:** step into the speaking place and tell the account, or remain seated while others fill the silence.
- **Arm:** receive it and hang it, or leave it in Mindia's hands and turn away. If Aluda cut it, carry it to the tower peg or take it back toward Mutsal's kin.
- **Feast:** guide the bull to the sacrifice place and strike, or lead it away and sheathe the sword. Show the blow without gore.

### Fairness rules

- Failing a timed input never makes a moral choice for the player.
- Before an irreversible act, the player can stop and step back.
- Skill creates advantages and costs. Conscience stays the player's decision.
- On defeat, restart from the last hearth or encounter checkpoint. Earlier decisions stay. Defeat creates no seventh ending.
- Offer difficulty and timing options, so slower players can finish the story.

## 4. Shared acts before the endings

All routes share these acts. NPCs remember each action.

### Prologue: A day in Shatili \[New\]

- **Where and when:** towers, roofs and nearby slopes in late summer.
- **Plot:** Aluda is at the height of his honor. Old enemy hands hang on his tower.
- **Actions:** climb between towers, hunt with Mindia, fence with the flat blade against Gela, teach Zezva to aim and feed Murana. Ushisha watches. Lela and Mother establish home life. The brother and nephews help with herds.
- **Purpose:** teach every basic skill and show what Aluda may lose.

### Act 1: Raid and duel \[Poem, expanded\]

- **Where:** sheepfolds, high pasture toward Arkhoti, then the crags.
- **Cause:** a messenger reports an attack on shepherds and stolen horses, including Aluda's. He leaves to recover them and answer the attack.
- **Tracking:** at dawn, follow signs. Birdcalls and still sheepfold dogs establish the hour. A river detour **\[New\]** lets Aluda meet Sanata, but gives the raiders better cover.
- **Combat 1:** Mutsal's brother. A short, sudden rifle fight. One good shot can end it, and careless exposure is dangerous.
- **Combat 2:** Mutsal, alone among the rocks. He fights because his brother lies dead. Phase one is movement and reloading. Phase two breaks cover; a scripted shot destroys Aluda's powder horn, but leaves enough powder to finish **\[Poem, expanded\]**. Phase three: Mutsal, mortally wounded, packs grass into the wound, fires again and throws his rifle toward Aluda **\[Poem\]**. The player cannot save him.
- **Decision:** spare or cut the hand. In either case Aluda also takes the brother's arm **\[Poem\]**. This keeps his conflict unfinished: even if he spares Mutsal's hand, he has not yet left the old custom.
- **If spared \[Poem\]:** Aluda weeps, leaves the weapons and covers the body.
- **If cut \[New\]:** he leaves the weapons, covers the body and carries both arms. The covering gesture feels at odds with the cut.

### Act 2: Night camp and dream \[Changed\]

- **Where:** a rock shelter on the way home.
- **Action:** tend the fire, check the weapon and keep watch before sleep.
- **Dream \[Poem, moved and expanded\]:** warriors gather around the dead. Mutsal puts a dagger in Aluda's hand and asks to be killed. Human flesh is served; imply it without gore.
- **Dream fight:** the Mutsal duel repeats in a silent mirror of Shatili. Attacking brings more rounds. Only the final dagger action records the decision.
- **Lower:** on waking, Aluda cannot see the trophies as before.
- **Strike:** he wakes resolved to trust custom. The dream changes his mind, never other people's knowledge.

### Act 3: Return and council \[Poem, expanded\]

- **Where:** past Imeda's fort crags to Shatili.
- **If spared and truthful:** Aluda praises Mutsal. Ushisha listens. Gela and the young men call Aluda a liar. Berdia is troubled by the praise. Mindia rebukes them and rides openly for proof.
- **If spared and silent \[New\]:** people assume the other hand was lost or will follow. Mindia notices the gap and rides quietly to protect his friend's name.
- **If cut:** the village celebrates. Mindia sees that Aluda takes no joy. Ushisha asks why a victor is silent about the man.
- **Village skill scene:** if truthful, Gela challenges Aluda. In a flat-blade bout, control him without injury until Lela or another woman stops it. Refusing to draw is also allowed. If silent or cut, this is Zezva's training bout instead.
- **Home:** Lela notices that Aluda cannot eat. Mother offers bread. He may share the dream; this does not alter his council record.

### Act 4: The arm \[Poem, expanded\]

- **Spared and truthful:** Mindia brings Mutsal's arm before the village.
- **Spared and silent \[New\]:** Mindia brings it privately at night. Only he and Aluda know when it was taken. Villagers who later see it believe Aluda brought it home and held it back.
- **Accept:** hang it. If Aluda stayed silent, the secret is intact.
- **Refuse:** Mindia, hurt, throws it into the gorge **\[Poem\]**. In the silent version he acts unseen.
- **If cut:** give it up by making a journey to Aiza, or hang it.
- **Playable journey:** a gorge climb (refused) or a trip to the crags (cut and given up). A refused arm can be recovered from the gorge as an optional deed. Failure returns Aluda to the climb. If he skips it, ravens take the arm.
- **Return meeting:** Aiza waits at the crags. Elmurza draws and tests Aluda with insults and feints. Hold restraint until Aiza stops him. This earns **Aiza's trust** if the arm arrives.

### Preparation: What helpers need

Side quests are optional. They add support but do not change the ending map.

- **Ushisha:** tell the truth at council, then repair a roof beside him and wait through his silence. He reveals he once spared a man **\[New\]**.
- **Sanata:** meet on the river detour, then gather herbs. She gives marked stones and treatment.
- **Gigia:** cross rough ground to Anatori and repair a memorial without taking grave goods. He shows an old pass path.
- **Lela:** gather wool and speak honestly with her. She makes felt cloaks.
- **Mindia:** hunt together and share the dream before the feast. This builds a **deep friendship**.

### Act 5: The shrine feast \[Poem, expanded\]

- **Where:** shrine ground in late autumn.
- **Plot:** Aluda brings a black young bull and asks Berdia to dedicate it to Mutsal, remembering his brother too. Berdia refuses. In the poem Aluda then kills the bull himself and prays for Mutsal.
- **Playable action:** calm the bull through a crowded path, then face Berdia. Hold your ground while Gela jostles you. No lethal combat occurs here: shedding a neighbor's blood would start another conflict and break the scene's logic.
- **Final action:** sacrifice or hold back. If Aluda holds back, Berdia treats the request as withdrawn.
- **Secret:** if Aluda sacrifices, his prayer shows that he honored a man whose arm hangs as his trophy.

## 5. Exile routes

All three begin with Berdia's call to exile Aluda, burn his property and drive off his herds **\[Poem, expanded\]**. Showing the destruction as playable scenes is new. Each route asks a different question, so each journey has its own conflict.

## Ending 1: The Exile

**Question:** can Aluda remain faithful to people who cast him out?

**Path:** spared, refused, sacrificed. **Season:** late autumn into winter.

### Plot

1. **Shatili, burning house:** the men carry out the sentence. If the arm was not returned, its gnawed remains reappear: from the raven if left in the gorge, or seized from Aluda if recovered. A dog refuses to eat them and howls, and children drag them with a hook **\[Poem, expanded\]**.
2. **Action:** lead the family out and save only a few loads: food, wool, medicine or a family keepsake. There is not time for all.
3. **Gorge road:** snow covers the way. The mother, Lela and children grieve.
4. **Imeda's fort crags \[New placement\]:** an avalanche zone. One person breaks trail while five follow.
5. **Stone hut:** wolves arrive at night.
6. **Ridge:** Aluda tells the women not to curse the community or shrine. He says farewell to the peaks, home and shrine. The family crosses, and distant weeping is heard **\[Poem\]**.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** grieves at the feast and cannot stop the sentence **\[Poem\]**. With deep friendship, he later leaves firewood on the road. He does not overturn the exile.
- **Berdia:** enforces the sentence without enjoying the family's pain.
- **Ushisha:** if trusted, quietly returns a keepsake. Otherwise he lowers his eyes.
- **Gela:** leads the destruction. If Aluda controlled him in the bout, Gela stops the men from mocking the children.
- **Zezva:** watches. If taught well, he brings dry kindling.
- **Lela:** organizes the children and keeps pace. **Mother:** worries who will help the Khevsurs if they need Aluda.
- **Family men:** the writer assigns the sixth traveller and shows which relatives stay.
- **Sanata, Gigia:** earned herbs or path knowledge help. **Murana:** warns of wolves.

### Combat and skill

- **Burning home:** no killing. Block falling beams, climb down with loads and push through without drawing on neighbors.
- **Trail:** choose firm footing, test slopes, hear cracking snow and stop the group in time.
- **Wolves at the hut:** a small pack, driven by hunger. Use fire, shield and calls to keep the circle closed. Win by driving them off, not killing every wolf.
- **Ridge standoff, only if the arm was never returned:** two Kist watchers fear an armed intruder. Hold fire and lower weapons while keeping the family behind cover. Aiming or advancing starts the standoff again.

**Skill changes:** food, injuries, saved keepsake and how much strength remains. With Aiza's trust, a guide may offer shelter beyond the ridge. The game closes before promising that life will be easy.

## Ending 2: The Broken Trophy

**Question:** can a man honor an enemy while displaying his hand?

**Path:** spared, accepted, sacrificed. **Season:** late autumn into early winter.

### Two versions

- **Truth told:** people call Aluda two-faced. He asked for proof, hung it and now mourns its owner.
- **Secret kept:** the prayer exposes the contradiction. Gela questions Mindia. Mindia must admit he brought the arm, or lie for Aluda. He tells the truth.

### Plot

1. **Feast to tower:** Berdia gives the sentence. Mindia doubts whether Aluda used his proof for pride.
2. **Burning tower:** Aluda can take down the arm. This is a new chance to repair what accepting it did.
3. **Choice of load:** the arm, extra food or medicine. This affects relationships and survival, not the ending.
4. **Gorge and Ardoti water:** Elmurza's small party has heard about the displayed arm and blocks the ford.
5. **Crags:** return the arm to Aiza if she agrees to meet, or keep fleeing.
6. **Winter ridge:** Mindia arrives only if their friendship was deep. He helps the children first and speaks to Aluda last.
7. **Last scene:** Aluda looks at the empty peg in memory. The family leaves with or without reconciliation.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** feels used. Aluda must show the change through action: carry the arm back and protect Mindia at the ford if he comes.
- **Berdia:** judges the offering, whichever version is true.
- **Gela:** says both men deceived the village. **Ushisha:** says a changing heart can look false from outside.
- **Lela:** stays, but asks why Aluda accepted the arm. **Mother:** begs him to put the family first.
- **Zezva:** is confused about whether trophies mean honor. His question follows the carried arm.
- **Aiza:** will receive the arm but does not praise a late return. **Elmurza:** treats the display as an insult.

### Combat and skill

- **Burning tower:** timed climbing with smoke and weak floors.
- **Ford:** three Kists. Use cover to protect the family while moving them across. Wounding or forcing back attackers ends the fight; extermination is unnecessary. With the arm, Aluda may expose himself to offer it. He must hold restraint through one feint.
- **If Mindia comes:** coordinate cover and movement. Friendly calls respond slowly until he regains trust during the fight.
- **Wolves:** they approach the dropped food stores in a night defense.

**Skill changes:** whether Mindia is wounded, supplies survive, the arm reaches Aiza and Mindia says farewell. Return can earn a truce for that crossing only.

## Ending 3: Late Repentance

**Question:** what can repentance repair after the harm is done?

**Path:** cut, sacrificed; any dream or arm action. **Season:** late autumn into early winter.

### Plot

1. **Feast:** Gela asks, "You took his hand, so why mourn him?" This is **\[New\]** dialogue.
2. **Sentence:** the village sees contradiction. Berdia sees a forbidden offering.
3. **Tower:** if the arm hangs there, recover it or leave it to burn. If already returned, Aiza has it.
4. **Exile road:** Elmurza tracks Aluda for the death and mutilation of his kinsman. The young nephew follows him.
5. **Mutso approach \[New detour\]:** weather forces both sides near the old defenses.
6. **The old crags:** avalanche debris reshapes the place of Mutsal's duel into narrow cover. The roles reverse: Aluda is now injured and short of powder. The nephew watches.
7. **After the duel:** Elmurza is disarmed and alive. Aluda may help him out of the cold or leave him with shelter. He cannot erase Mutsal's death.
8. **Departure:** the family crosses. Elmurza does not call this forgiveness.

If the writer cannot make the Mutso-to-crags detour fit the map, use a new crag site with similar cover.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** respects the courage but asks why Aluda changed only now. With deep friendship, he guards the rear.
- **Berdia:** gives the same ruling. **Ushisha:** says regret is real even when late. **Gela:** feels his mockery justified.
- **Lela:** accepts the journey but wants no more speeches that endanger the children. **Mother:** counts the cost in food and warmth.
- **Zezva:** sees a trophy-taker begin to doubt.
- **Aiza:** if the arm was returned, allows medicine or passage but remains grieving. If not, she demands it back. If it burned, she gives no help.
- **Elmurza:** restrains his men if the arm was returned, but still demands a personal fight.

### Combat and skill

- **Rear guard:** delay pursuers with positions and blocked paths while family moves.
- **Elmurza, rifle phase:** use remembered reload sounds and fewer shots.
- **Close phase:** sword and shield on ice. Disarm through timed counters; the story fight ends before a killing blow.
- **Rescue:** pull Elmurza or the nephew from unstable snow, if chosen, while watching footing.

**Skill changes:** wounds, losses of supplies, shelter and whether the nephew leaves with fear or doubt. No performance restores full trust.

## 6. Shatili routes

Holding back saves Aluda's home. The cost unfolds from winter to spring. All three are **\[New\]**.

## Ending 4: The Silent Secret

**Question:** how much will Aluda lose to protect a false honor?

**Path:** spared, struck, silent, accepted, held back.

### Plot

1. **Autumn evening:** Shatili praises Aluda. Zezva asks to hear how he took the second hand.
2. **Early winter, hearth:** Mindia refuses to repeat the heroic story. He will keep silent but will not invent details.
3. **Midwinter, pasture shelter:** wolves attack penned animals. Aluda and Mindia must fight together while barely speaking.
4. **Winter night, towers:** Murana or a family member hears Aluda sleepwalking toward the arm. Climb after him through a dream that overlaps the real roofs.
5. **Late spring, ford:** Aiza arrives with a small escort to ask for the remains.
6. **Night watch:** Aluda chooses by action to give her the arm in secret or keep it. Neither changes the ending.
7. **If given:** replace it with an empty wrapped shape. Gela notices movement, and Aluda must protect Aiza's escape without exposing or killing anyone.
8. **If kept:** Elmurza's party raids the pasture. Aiza's request has failed.
9. **Spring feast table:** Gela asks for the story. Aluda tells the praised version or leaves in silence. Mindia walks away. Only Mindia knows how the hand reached the tower. Aiza knows it was returned if that happened, but not how Shatili was deceived.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** loyal in danger, distant at home. He never exposes the secret.
- **Berdia:** praises obedience. **Gela:** admires, then watches closely if Aluda moves the arm. **Ushisha:** senses a gap but learns no fact.
- **Lela:** notices fear and asks for honesty; the truth is not revealed to her. **Mother:** welcomes safety but sees him stop sleeping. **Brother and nephews:** defend his reputation.
- **Zezva:** copies the praised story, increasing the harm of silence.
- **Aiza:** wants return, not Aluda's comfort. **Elmurza:** believes Aluda mutilated Mutsal. **Sanata** and **Gigia:** help with injuries or paths without learning the secret.

### Combat and skill

- **Winter wolves:** gates, fire and calls; protect people and herd together.
- **Roof pursuit:** careful climbing over ice, with dream shapes that never replace safe footholds without warning.
- **Night watch:** escort or intercept through towers using sound and shadow. No villager dies.
- **Spring raid, either arm action:** after secret return, hostile men who never received Aiza's message attack; success can spread the message and end it earlier. If kept, Elmurza fights longer to recover the arm.

**Skill changes:** herd losses, Mindia's injury, Zezva's safety and the length of the raid. **Final image:** the village calls Aluda's name while Mindia's seat stays empty.

## Ending 5: The Old Law

**Question:** what happens when each hand creates another avenger?

**Path:** cut, struck, hung, held back.

### Plot

1. **Autumn:** Gela and the young men honor Aluda. Berdia gives him a place of respect.
2. **Winter, roofs and hearths:** Aluda trains them. Zezva becomes skilled. Gigia may show the graves left by older feuds.
3. **Winter hunt:** wolves cut off Mindia and Zezva near the rocks. Aluda rescues them.
4. **Late spring, pasture:** Elmurza leads a retaliation raid with Kists. Their aim is revenge and Mutsal's remains.
5. **Ardoti ford:** pursue the raiders. Mindia warns that the party is being drawn away from Shatili.
6. **Return to towers:** a second small group reaches the herds and approach. Defend families and gates.
7. **Crags:** Aluda follows Elmurza to the place of Mutsal's death. Their duel repeats its rifle rhythm.
8. **Last act:** Elmurza dies after throwing away his rifle so a rival cannot take it. The nephew watches. Zezva raises a knife toward the right hand. Aluda may stop him, but his own history makes the command hollow.
9. **Autumn after:** Shatili celebrates. Across the ridge, the nephew cleans a rifle. Aluda sees the next feud begin.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** fights bravely and follows custom, but grows cautious when children join it.
- **Berdia:** supports defense. **Ushisha:** recalls old losses. **Gela:** pushes for pursuit and may abandon his post.
- **Lela:** gathers children and demands that Zezva stay back. **Mother:** treats each celebration as fear before another loss. **Brother and nephews:** guard stock and gates; the writer chooses a lasting injury.
- **Zezva:** wants Aluda's approval. His final action reflects training: prior restraint lessons make him hesitate; trophy lessons make him act.
- **Aiza:** refuses help and asks for the dead. **Elmurza:** fights as a grieving relative. **Nephew:** survives and remembers. **Sanata:** treats the wounded but asks how many more there will be.

### Combat and skill

- **Pasture:** moving rifle fight among animals; avoid hitting shepherds.
- **Ford:** cover, flanking and calls. A reckless chase makes the tower defense harder.
- **Tower defense:** ladders, narrow entries, sword and shield. A few raiders are dangerous.
- **Elmurza duel:** all three Mutsal phases, but he anticipates Aluda's old tactics.
- **Training:** lessons teach guard, retreat and calls, rather than only stronger attacks.

**Skill changes:** survivors, injuries, herds and Zezva's condition. Even if Zezva stops, the nephew still carries revenge.

## Ending 6: The Hand on the Tower

**Question:** can regret change a man who does not act at the decisive moment?

**Path:** every held-back path other than endings 4 and 5.

### Opening versions

- **Spared and refused:** no arm hangs there. An empty peg becomes the "hand" Aluda sees. He refused the trophy but abandoned the offering.
- **Spared and accepted:** the arm hangs. He either spoke truthfully or hid the truth without fully accepting custom.
- **Cut and gave up:** the peg is empty; returning the arm did not restore Mutsal.
- **Cut, hung and lowered the dagger:** the arm remains, though conscience has begun to oppose it.

### Plot

1. **Feast aftermath:** Aluda keeps his home. People respond to what he told and displayed.
2. **Early winter, tower:** Lela asks what holding back was meant to protect.
3. **Midwinter, Imeda's crags:** Gela's hunting party is caught by snow. Aluda must rescue the man who most pressed custom on him.
4. **Shelter:** wolves surround the injured. Aluda defends them through the night.
5. **Late spring, pasture:** a new raid begins from need and local hostility. Elmurza joins only if the arm remains in Shatili.
6. **Crags:** Aluda corners Mutsal's young nephew, who is frightened and low on powder.
7. **Actionable mercy:** disarm him, then escort him away or keep him captive for elders to decide. Killing is outside this ending.
8. **Return:** Aluda saves a life or limits harm, but still lives beside what he left undone.
9. **Last image:** he reaches toward the arm or empty peg, then stops. If an arm hangs there, he may take it down for a later return. The ending remains open.

### NPC behaviour

- **Mindia:** supports survival if friendship was deep, but asks why Aluda retreated. If the arm was hung, he questions what the proof was for.
- **Berdia:** welcomes submission. **Gela:** treats it as proof custom won; after rescue, he mocks less but need not change belief.
- **Ushisha:** if trusted, shares his own unresolved mercy.
- **Lela:** values the saved home yet refuses to call him at peace. **Mother:** gives bread and urges him to live. **Brother and nephews:** return to ordinary work with him.
- **Zezva:** notices the difference between Aluda's words and tower. His response follows earlier training.
- **Aiza:** trusts him only if the arm was returned. Otherwise she asks again in spring. **Elmurza:** pursues an unreturned arm. **Sanata** and **Gigia:** offer aid if their quests were completed.

### Combat and skill

- **Avalanche rescue:** locate calls, probe snow, dig and brace unstable debris.
- **Wolves:** hold a shelter entrance and manage firewood.
- **Spring raid:** defend shepherds and recover animals.
- **Nephew encounter:** block panicked attacks, break his aim and control him without lethal force.

**Skill changes:** Gela's recovery, herd losses, injuries and the nephew's condition. Success shows what Aluda can still do, without reversing the feast.

## 7. Realism rules and build order

### Combat logic

- Every fight needs a cause, a goal for each side and a reason to stop.
- Raids involve a few named or distinct people, not waves of identical enemies.
- People retreat, surrender, protect kin and run short of powder.
- Village disputes are controlled and non-lethal. A killing there would require a new blood-feud story.
- Wolves can be driven away. Weather cannot be defeated; the player endures or avoids it.
- Winter has no large raids. Pursuit in early winter uses a small party before travel becomes impractical. Writers must check each route against the season.
- Wounds affect movement, aim or climbing until treated.
- Kists have families, motives and differing opinions. They are not a monster faction.

### Keeping NPCs consistent

For every reaction, writers should record what that person saw, heard and wants. An NPC cannot react to a secret they never learned. Travel and injuries also limit where they can appear. An NPC lost or injured during play needs an alternate line and action in later scenes.

### Cultural and story review

- Ask Khevsur and Kist/Ingush culture specialists to review clothing, faith, mourning, hospitality, justice and names.
- Treat new customs, guest arrangements and fictional places as drafts.
- Show mutilation through framing, cloth or silhouette.
- Keep Berdia's fear understandable: he believes breaking sacred law endangers everyone.
- Do not make mercy automatically safe or custom automatically foolish.
